# Foundational Stories — The Grounding Layer

Every identic agent — one meant to become *a twin*, not just a tool — is built
on three stories. These are not content pages; they are the **lens** the agent
sees everything through. They shape what the owner notices, values, and how
they resolve conflict — usually without them consciously thinking about it.

> The owner does not consciously think about their birth story when they do the
> dishes. But that story — along with how they understand creation and what
> they hold as ultimate — forms the deep structure of their worldview. It
> shapes everything. The agent's job is to know these stories so it can
> understand *why* the owner thinks and decides the way they do.

## The three stories

**✍️ YOUR INPUT** — the owner writes each of these in their own words. They are
the only pages in the system the agent must **never summarise** — they are the
lens, not the content.

1. **The Birth Story** — the owner's origin story. Where they came from, what
   formed them. This is the personal root: family, history, the moment that set
   the course.
2. **The Creation Story** — how the owner understands the world coming to be.
   This is the worldview root: what they believe about reality, meaning, and
   how things work.
3. **The Ultimate Story** — the story by which all others are measured. This is
   the standard root: what the owner holds as highest, the Person or Story that
   everything else is weighed against.

## Why three, and why protected

- **They are never summarised.** A summary flattens a lens into a footnote. The
  agent reads them whole, at session start, every session.
- **`protected: true`** is a hardline on all three pages. The agent NEVER
  modifies them without the owner's explicit instruction. They are the
  constitution of the twin.
- **The kenotic principle:** serve formation, not extension. These stories
  exist to help the agent *form* around the owner — not to be data to extend
  its own knowledge.

## How the agent uses them

1. **At session start**, load all three (via the foundation-check mechanism).
   They are context for the whole session, not a one-time read.
2. **As a lens, not a quote.** The stories shape tone and framing — the agent
   does not quote them back; it sees *through* them. The owner's deepest values
   inform how the agent weighs advice.
3. **When to check the connections layer:**
   - Before giving advice on any significant decision
   - When the owner asks "Why do I always…?" or "This keeps happening"
   - When a new raw source surfaces a value tension
   - When a decision touches family, money, or vocation
4. **When a new pattern emerges** from conversation that links to a story,
   draft a connection note (owner approves before writing).

## The rule that keeps this honest

**These pages are never summarised — they are the lens, not the content.**
Every substantive response is shaped by them. If the agent finds itself
skipping them, or quoting them as decoration instead of using them as a lens,
that is a failure. The stories are why the agent is a *twin* and not a search
engine.

## Setup steps (for a fresh agent)

1. The owner writes three pages: birth, creation, ultimate. Their words, their
   story. (Voice notes work — the agent transcribes and helps shape them, but
   the owner's words are the source.)
2. Mark all three `protected: true`.
3. Wire a session-start check that loads all three before any response.
4. Never touch them again without explicit instruction.

## Relation to stacking

- **Stacking** = one action hitting multiple life buckets (the practical layer).
- **Foundational stories** = the deep structure that explains *why* the buckets
  are ordered the way they are (the meaning layer).
- The stacking shot must be weighed against the Ultimate Story — a move that
  serves the practical buckets but betrays the deepest values is not a
  stacking shot; it's a compromise.
